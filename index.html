Reciprocity & Quiet-Site Receiver Sensitivity

By electromagnetic reciprocity, transmit efficiency and elevation directivity apply identically on receive. On quiet high-altitude sites and upper HF bands (especially 17m), external atmospheric noise drops near the receiver's thermal noise floor. Higher radiation efficiency (+0.31 dB on 40m; 83.6% on 17m) and 17m's low-angle pattern discrimination (86.5% below 30°, rejecting high-angle local NVIS QRM and thunderstorms) directly improve received SNR.

Why Box 1 & Box 3 Protect Receiver Dynamic Range

Box 1 (Topology High-Pass) pairs a 34.52 pF series barrier (|X_c| = 4,611 Ω at 1 MHz) with a 1.37 µH shunt inductor to ground (|X_L| = 8.6 Ω at 1 MHz), creating a steep 2nd-order high-pass divider yielding −87.1 dB AM broadcast suppression and −20 to −49 dB out-of-band HF attenuation. On 40m, Box 3's 183.5 pF capacitor presents ~4× higher low-frequency barrier impedance (867 Ω vs. 225 Ω at 1 MHz) than Box 2's 708.5 pF.

Electrical Principle — Over-Loaded Mid-Height Inductance + Base Series Capacitance

On 80m (λ = 82.135 m at 3.650 MHz), a vertical mast under 13 m is electrically short (~0.14λ–0.15λ): a standard base-loaded 10.35 m wire yields only R_rad = 7.1 Ω. Placing an inductor at mid-height lifts the high-current belly upward, roughly doubling radiation resistance.
